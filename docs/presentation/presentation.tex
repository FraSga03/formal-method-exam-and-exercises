\section{Context}

\begin{frame}{Process mining in one slide}
An information system records every state change it performs. That record is an
\emph{event log}: for each case, a finite sequence of \emph{(case, activity,
timestamp)} events.
\bigskip
\begin{description}
  \item[Discovery] derive a model --- here a Petri net --- from the log alone.
  \item[Conformance] measure how well a model and a log agree.
  \item[Verification] decide whether a stated property holds of every case.
\end{description}
\bigskip
A designed model says how work \emph{should} proceed. A discovered model says
how it \emph{did}. The gap between them is the object of study.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The question}
\begin{itemize}
  \item An IT service desk records every state change of every incident.
  \item The process is \emph{documented}. What was actually \emph{executed}?
  \bigskip
  \item Volvo IT Belgium, VINST system, 2010--2012.
  \item Three logs: incidents, open problems, closed problems.
  \item A standard benchmark, so the results are comparable with the literature.
  \bigskip
  \item Nothing here is read off a dashboard by eye: every figure shown is
        produced by code and pinned by a regression test.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Discovery}

\begin{frame}{Three discovery algorithms}
\begin{description}
  \item[Alpha] Ordering relations from directly-follows pairs. Assumes a
        complete, noise-free log. Cannot represent short loops.
  \item[Heuristics] Frequency-weighted, with a dependency threshold. Filters
        infrequent behaviour instead of modelling it. Robust to noise.
  \item[Inductive] Recursive partitioning into a process tree, converted to a
        net. Guarantees soundness and, at zero noise, perfect fitness.
\end{description}
\bigskip
All three are run on the full incident log, under both labellings, from the same
loaded bundle --- so the six rows on the next slides differ only in the
algorithm and the labelling.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Four dimensions, not one}
\begin{description}
  \item[Fitness] Can the model replay what happened?
  \item[Precision] Does it forbid what never happened?
  \item[Generalisation] Will it hold for future cases?
  \item[Simplicity] Can a human read it?
\end{description}
\bigskip
They trade off. Maximising any one alone is trivial ---
a model permitting everything fits perfectly and explains nothing; a model
enumerating the observed traces is perfectly precise and predicts nothing.
\bigskip
Hence a table, not a winner.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Discovery results}
\begin{center}\scriptsize\input{../tables/discovery.tex}\end{center}
\bigskip
\small
Inductive buys perfect fitness with permissiveness and the largest net.
Heuristics gives the best balance. Alpha collapses.
\bigskip

Moving from 4 labels to 13 lowers precision for every algorithm and roughly
triples the net --- there is simply more distinct behaviour to explain.
\end{frame}

\bigskip

\bigskip

$(a,a)$ and provably cannot represent such loops.
\bigskip
A known theoretical boundary, demonstrated on a real log.

\section{Verification}

\begin{frame}{Temporal logic over \emph{finite} traces}
A case is a finite sequence of events, so the semantics are $\text{LTL}_f$.
\bigskip
\begin{align*}
  \pi, i \models \mathbf{F}\varphi &\iff \exists j \geq i.\ \pi, j \models \varphi \\
  \pi, i \models \mathbf{G}\varphi &\iff \forall j \geq i.\ \pi, j \models \varphi \\
  \pi, i \models \mathbf{X}\varphi &\iff i+1 < n \ \textbf{and}\ \pi, i{+}1 \models \varphi
\end{align*}
\bigskip
\alert{$\mathbf{X}$ is \emph{strong}: false at the last position.} Remember this
for two slides' time.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The property library}
Eight properties, each a real formula evaluated per case.
\bigskip
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Termination & $\mathbf{F}(\textit{completed}_*)$ \\
Resolution & $\mathbf{G}(\textit{in\_progress} \rightarrow \mathbf{F}\,\textit{completed}_*)$ \\
No work after closure & $\mathbf{G}(\textit{closed} \rightarrow \neg\mathbf{F}\,\textit{in\_progress})$ \\
Queued work picked up & $\mathbf{G}(\textit{queued} \rightarrow \mathbf{F}\,\textit{in\_progress})$ \\
\alert{Formal closure} & $\mathbf{G}(\textit{in\_progress} \rightarrow \mathbf{F}\,\textit{closed})$ \\
\alert{Queueing precedes work} & $(\neg\,\textit{in\_progress}) \ \mathbf{U}\ \textit{queued}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\bigskip\normalsize

The last two encode the \emph{documented} procedure. They are expected to fail.
The size of the gap is the result.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Verification results}
\begin{center}\small\input{../tables/ltl.tex}\end{center}
\vspace{-0.3em}
\begin{center}
\small Every case of the incident log, every property. No sampling.
\end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Reading the table}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cases do not disappear.} Termination, resolution and recovery
        from \act{Wait - User} each hold for essentially every case.
  \item \textbf{Closure is not universal.} A quarter of worked incidents never
        reach \act{Completed+Closed}; they settle as \act{Completed+In Call}.
        Defect or undocumented route --- the analysis establishes only that both
        exist at scale.
  \item \textbf{Queueing before work all but never happens.} The one property
        stating the documented procedure is the one that fails.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Findings}

\begin{frame}{Finding 1 --- rework dominates}
\begin{center}
\Large
Fewer than half of all events are the \emph{first} occurrence\\
of their activity within the case.
\end{center}
\bigskip
\begin{itemize}
  \item Over half of all recorded work is repetition.
  \item Completion is near perfect; variability is moderate.
  \item Rework is the single component dragging the score down --- and the
        largest available lever.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{One score, and what it is made of}
\begin{center}\small\input{../tables/health.tex}\end{center}
\bigskip
\small
Four normalised components: completion, first-occurrence share, one minus the
variant ratio, and throughput against a fixed horizon.
\bigskip

The weights and the horizon are \emph{stipulated}. The components are
meaningful; the aggregate only comparatively --- and comparison is what it is
used for.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Finding 2 --- push to front}
\begin{center}
\Large
$(\neg\,\textit{in\_progress}) \ \mathbf{U}\ \textit{queued}$ \quad holds for a
small minority of cases.
\end{center}
\bigskip
\begin{itemize}
  \item The documented procedure: queue the incident, then work it.
  \item What happens: work begins without the incident ever being queued.
  \item Established by \emph{formal verification over every case} --- not by
        sampling, not by eyeballing variants.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The same pattern in the transitions}
\begin{columns}[c]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[height=0.72\textheight]{transition_heatmap.png}
\end{center}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
\small
Directly-follows frequencies, normalised per row: given this activity, what
comes next?
\bigskip

One column absorbs nearly every row --- \act{Accepted+In Progress}. Work is what
follows everything.
\bigskip

The \act{Queued+Awaiting Assignment} \emph{column} stays faint: almost nothing
flows \emph{into} the queue. Push to front, visible without a formula.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Two methods, one conclusion}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Verification}\\[0.4em]
$\mathbf{G}(\textit{closed} \rightarrow \neg\mathbf{F}\,\textit{in\_progress})$
fails for a set of cases: work resumes after formal closure.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Performance}\\[0.4em]
\act{Completed+Closed} appears in the waiting-time table at all --- meaning
closure events that were \emph{not} the last event of their case.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\bigskip\bigskip
\begin{center}
Independent methods agreeing is stronger than either alone.
\end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The strong-\texorpdfstring{$\mathbf{X}$}{X} trap}
Written the natural infinite-trace way:
\[
  \mathbf{G}(\textit{closed} \rightarrow \mathbf{X}\,\mathbf{G}\,\neg\,\textit{in\_progress})
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alert{26.2\%}
\]
Written without the next operator:
\[
  \mathbf{G}(\textit{closed} \rightarrow \neg\mathbf{F}\,\textit{in\_progress})
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \textbf{98.4\%}
\]
\bigskip
Most cases \emph{end} with \act{Completed+Closed}. $\mathbf{X}$ is false there,
so every such case falsifies the first formula for reasons having nothing to do
with Volvo IT.
\bigskip
\begin{center}
A factor of four apart. Only one of them is about the process.
\end{center}
\end{frame}

\section{Implementation}

\begin{frame}{Four layers, no state between them}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\textbf{The packages}
\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{logs}] loading, schema detection, filters
  \item[\texttt{mining}] discovery, conformance, performance
  \item[\texttt{verification}] $\text{LTL}_f$ per trace
  \item[\texttt{analysis}] score, anomalies, prediction
\end{description}
\smallskip
\texttt{ui}, \texttt{reporting} and \texttt{scripts} sit on top; nothing depends
on \texttt{ui}.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\textbf{Consequences}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Every function takes a loaded log and returns a value --- no module
        state, no cache
  \item Dashboard callbacks are plain functions, called directly by 202 tests
  \item Two browser sessions are independent
  \item Every table and figure on these slides is generated from code
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Two decisions worth defending}
\textbf{The loader adds columns; it never drops any.}\\
Normalisation \emph{adds} the XES-standard names to a copy of the raw frame. The
support-line, ownership and country attributes survive --- reducing each event to
three columns, as is common, would make them unrecoverable without reparsing.
\bigskip

\textbf{A named property is not a special case.}\\
The eight properties are declared as data --- name, formula, measured ratio ---
and evaluated by the same function that evaluates a formula typed into the
dashboard. The recorded ratios are asserted by the test suite, so a change that
moved a published figure would fail the build rather than quietly update the
report.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Threats to validity}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Conformance is computed on a \emph{sample}; alignment-based computation
        does not scale to the full log.
  \item Token-based replay is optimistic on unsound nets. Alignments agree on
        the sample.
  \item The composite score's weights are \emph{stipulated}. Components are
        meaningful; the aggregate only comparatively.
  \item \act{Unmatched+Unmatched} is an export artefact, not work. Retained so
        its prevalence is visible.
  \item We observe what was \emph{recorded}. Poor recording discipline looks
        like rework.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Conclusions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Rework dominates} --- over half of recorded work is repetition.
  \item \textbf{Documented $\neq$ executed} --- push-to-front proved formally
        over every case.
  \item \textbf{Alpha is inapplicable here} --- predicted by length-one loops,
        not an empirical surprise.
\end{enumerate}
\bigskip
\textbf{Next:} the organisational attributes are already loaded --- handover
analysis across support lines, push-to-front quantified by organisation, and
incident versus problem as interacting processes.
\end{frame}
